**Chronicle** (in Portuguese, *crônica*): A literary genre. A short narrative, told in chronological order, with one goal: to please and inform the reader within a single context, building a familiarity between writer and reader. The first chronicle is attributed to the [Journal des Débats, in 1800](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k415703q).

**Terminal:** From the Latin *Terminus*, the name of the Roman god of boundaries, and also the name of the boundaries themselves. We adopted it, playfully, to mean any limit, physical or metaphorical. An electrical terminal, for example, is the point where an electrical circuit ends — from there it became natural to call the point of communication, the boundary between machine and human being, a terminal.

With that in mind, I concluded that, on the young-adult internet, we are all chroniclers. Our searches, our posts, our social media, our photos, our videos, our likes are nothing more than a chronicle: we write chronicles about ourselves all the time. I decided to write mine, telling the story of a young old-timer, child of a rain of green letters on a dark background, someone who came to understand one thing above all: the wish to tell stories.

But why let **one more dev** start **one more blog**?

Some would say it was destiny, others would say it was genetic and social engineering. After all, what stories could a Nilton — perhaps the fourth or fifth Nilton in his family, son and grandson of writers — tell? What stories could a Nilton, grandson of an Indigenous woman kidnapped from her tribe, tell? What stories could a Nilton tell through code and colored lines?

Well, let's see. My job is to write, and yours is to answer those questions.
